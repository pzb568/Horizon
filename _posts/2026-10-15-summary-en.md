### [PNAS paper shows self‑organizing networks match backpropagation performance](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x6hmyg/how_artificial_neural_networks_build_their_own/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new PNAS paper demonstrates that randomly interconnected neuron‑like units governed by shared plasticity rules can self‑assemble gradient‑learning circuits that achieve classification accuracy comparable to networks trained with backpropagation. The study shows these self‑organized motifs can learn pattern‑recognition and image‑classification tasks without explicitly designing a learning circuit. The authors stress the work is a proof‑of‑concept for biologically plausible learning, not evidence that the brain uses this mechanism, and note that stability across different initial connectivities remains an open question.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Dorian182 · Oct 15, 10:01

**「Background: Biologically Plausible Gradient Learning」** Backpropagation, the dominant training algorithm for artificial neural networks, relies on non‑local error signals and symmetric forward‑backward weight transport, features that are not observed in biological synapses. Consequently, a line of research has explored alternative, biologically plausible mechanisms that use only locally available activity to drive weight updates, such as self‑supervised learning, target‑propagation, and other local‑rule frameworks, many of which have demonstrated performance comparable to standard backpropagation on downstream tasks. The new PNAS study builds on this tradition by showing that randomly connected neuron‑like units governed by shared plasticity rules can self‑assemble gradient‑learning motifs that achieve backprop‑level accuracy on pattern‑recognition and image‑classification benchmarks.

**「Impact」** The results give AI researchers a concrete example of a local‑rule‑based training paradigm that can rival backpropagation, encouraging exploration of architectures that rely on self‑organization rather than global error signals.
