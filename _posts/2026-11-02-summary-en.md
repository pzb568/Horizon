### [OpenAI CEO apologizes to mathematicians but pledges continued AI math releases](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/sam-altman-mathematicians-math-slop) ⭐️ 7.0/10

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman apologized on a podcast to mathematicians who felt disrupted by recent AI‑generated mathematics. He said the apologies do not change OpenAI’s plan to keep publishing AI‑produced math results, arguing they advance humanity’s understanding of the universe. The statement follows earlier claims that OpenAI’s agents tackled the Navier‑Stokes problem and the release of hundreds of unreviewed AI proofs that drew criticism from leading mathematicians.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 1, 02:54

**「Navier–Stokes problem and OpenAI’s prior claim」** The Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness question is one of the seven Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize problems, asking whether smooth solutions always exist for the three‑dimensional fluid equations. In August 2024 OpenAI announced that a swarm of roughly 10,000 AI agents, orchestrated by an internal model described as more capable than GPT‑6 Astra, had produced a solution and generated hundreds of AI‑written mathematical proofs that were released without peer review, prompting criticism from leading mathematicians.

**「Impact」** Continuing to release unreviewed AI‑generated proofs will require mathematicians and journals to devote additional effort to verification, potentially slowing the integration of AI contributions into formal research and increasing the workload for peer review.

### [AI tokenizer tax can raise token costs up to 17× for some languages](https://clear-languages.org/blog/the-tokenizer-tax) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A CLEAR research report finds that the tokenizers used by mainstream AI models are heavily optimized for English and other high‑resource languages. As a result, the same sentence can require up to 17 times more tokens in certain low‑resource languages—for example, English needs 13 tokens, Malayalam 26, and Amharic 78. The higher token count directly inflates usage costs, adds latency, and reduces the effective context window for multilingual LLM applications. This finding is based on analysis of roughly 200 languages and reflects a current limitation rather than a new model feature.

telegram · zaihuapd · Nov 1, 16:06

**「Tokenization and cost model basics」** Large language models charge users based on the number of tokens processed, where a token is a piece of text produced by the model’s tokenizer. Tokenizers split input strings into sub‑word units, and their rules are usually trained on high‑resource languages such as English, giving those languages a more compact token representation. When the same semantic content is tokenized in lower‑resource languages, the tokenizer often produces many more sub‑words, inflating the token count and therefore the API cost and latency.

**「Impact」** Developers and organizations that bill LLM usage by token must budget significantly more for non‑English inputs, potentially up to 17 × the cost of English text, and may experience slower response times. They may need to adopt alternative tokenization strategies, limit multilingual queries, or adjust pricing models to mitigate the &quot;tokenizer tax.&quot;
